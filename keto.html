What is Nutritional Ketosis?

►

Nutritional ketosis is a safe, natural metabolic state where your body primarily burns fat for fuel instead of glucose (sugar). When carbohydrate intake is very low, the liver converts fat (from your diet and your body stores) into molecules called ketones. These ketones are then used by the brain and body as a clean and efficient energy source.

This is different from diabetic ketoacidosis, which is a dangerous medical emergency related to a lack of insulin in Type 1 diabetes.

The "Caloric Bypass": Myth vs. Reality

►

The term "caloric bypass" can be misleading. Calories don't disappear. The "bypass" refers to the unique metabolic pathway of Medium-Chain Triglycerides (MCTs).

  • Most Fats (LCTs): Go through the lymphatic system, are circulated, and are easily stored as body fat.
  • MCTs: "Bypass" this route and are sent directly to the liver. In the liver, they are rapidly converted into ketones for immediate energy.

So, MCTs are far less likely to be stored as fat and are preferentially used for energy, which supports a ketogenic state.

The MCT Family

C8 (Caprylic Acid): The Most Ketogenic

►

C8 is considered the gold standard. It is the fastest to be converted into ketones by the liver, providing rapid mental and physical energy. Pure C8 MCT oil is popular for this reason.

C10 (Capric Acid)

►

C10 is also highly effective, just slightly slower to metabolize than C8. Most standard MCT oils are a blend of C8 and C10, offering a slightly more sustained energy release.

C12 (Lauric Acid): The "Outlier"

►

C12 is the primary fat in coconut oil (around 47%). While technically an MCT, it behaves more like a long-chain fat (LCT). It's processed much more slowly and is not very ketogenic. While C12 has other health benefits (like being antimicrobial), it doesn't contribute much to ketone production.

Common Dietary Sources

MCT Oil Supplement

The most concentrated source. Usually 100% C8 or a C8/C10 blend. Designed specifically for ketone production.

Coconut Oil

A good whole-food source, but as the chart shows, it's mostly C12 (Lauric Acid). Only about 15% of coconut oil is the more ketogenic C8/C10.

Goat Cheese & Dairy

Goat and sheep milk (and cheeses) contain C10 (Capric Acid), which is where it gets its name. Grass-fed butter also contains some MCTs. These are much less concentrated sources than oils.

The Three Pillars of a Ketogenic Strategy

1. Carb Restriction

This is the most critical part. You must restrict carbohydrates (typically 20-50g/day) to force your body to switch from burning glucose to burning fat. Without this, ketosis won't happen.

2. Intermittent Fasting

A 12-16 hour daily fast (like the timeline above) helps deplete your liver glycogen stores more quickly, accelerating the switch to ketosis each day.

3. MCT Inclusion

Once carbs are low, adding MCTs (1-2 tbsp/day) provides a direct, fast fuel source for ketone production. This can boost energy, reduce "keto flu" symptoms, and deepen your state of ketosis.

A Note on Polyphenols

Polyphenols (found in berries, green tea, olive oil, dark chocolate) are powerful antioxidants. While they don't directly generate ketones like MCTs, they are excellent for supporting overall metabolic health, reducing inflammation, and protecting your cells. They are a supportive part of a healthy diet, not a primary driver of ketosis.

The benefits of ketosis may go deeper than just fuel. This section touches on the advanced topic of how ketones can affect your cells, specifically the mitochondria, which are the "power plants" of your cells.

Key Question: Do Ketones Multiply Mitochondria?

This is a key area of cutting-edge research. The short answer is that ketosis appears to trigger a process that builds new, more efficient mitochondria.

This process is called mitochondrial biogenesis.

When your body runs on ketones (a state achieved by fasting or a ketogenic diet), it acts as a mild, beneficial "stress" on your cells. This stress signals the cells to adapt by:

  • Building new mitochondria.
  • Improving the efficiency of existing mitochondria.
  • Cleaning up damaged mitochondria (autophagy).

More and healthier mitochondria mean your body can produce more energy more efficiently. This is a primary reason why ketosis is being studied for longevity, athletic endurance, and neurodegenerative conditions.
