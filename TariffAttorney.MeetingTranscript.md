Now we're going to distinguish between different categories of those impacted.
*   **Importer of Record (IOR):** The entity that filed the entry and paid the IEEPA duties directly to CBP. They're named on the entry summary. ### CAPE Submission (Phase 1)
**Matthew Silverman:**
CAPE launched around April 20th. It's rolling out in phases. Right now we're in **Phase 1**.
Phase 1 covers:
1.  **Unliquidated entries:** Entries where Customs has not finalized the duty calculation (usually takes about 300 days).
2.  **Entries liquidated within the previous 80 days.**
Phase 1 is expected to cover about 63% of IEEPA entries.
**Phase 2** is unknown as of today, but will likely include entries liquidated more than 80 days ago and more complicated entries with "stacked" duties (ADD/CVD). We don't advise clients to just sit on their hands and wait for Phase 2. There is another mechanism: **filing a protest**.

### Filing a Protest
**Matthew Silverman:**
If your entries have been liquidated for more than 80 days but less than 180 days, you should look at filing an administrative protest with US Customs. This window is strict and non-extendable. You're essentially making the same argument you would in CAPE. This allows you to not have to wait for Phase 2. If you miss your 180-day deadline to file a protest, you may be out of luck. It's possible to file with the US Court of International Trade (CIT), but usually only if you've already filed a protest and it was denied. If you're just missing administrative deadlines, the CIT is not going to look kindly on you.
