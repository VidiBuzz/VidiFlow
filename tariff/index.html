What Happened

"We decide whether the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) authorizes the President to impose tariffs. ... The President asserts the extraordinary power to unilaterally impose tariffs of unlimited amount, duration, and scope. In light of the breadth, history, and constitutional context of that asserted authority, he must identify clear congressional authorization to exercise it." — Chief Justice John Roberts, Opinion of the Court, Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, 607 U.S. ___ (2026)

⚖

The Supreme Court Ruled

In a 6-3 decision, the Court held that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs. Chief Justice Roberts wrote that "the terms of IEEPA do not authorize tariffs" and that Congress alone has the power to "lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises" under Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution.

💰

$175 Billion in Refunds

CBP estimates that over $166 billion in IEEPA tariffs were unlawfully collected from more than 330,000 importers across 53 million entries. This includes the "Fentanyl Tariffs" on Canada, Mexico, and China, and the "Liberation Day" reciprocal tariffs on virtually every country.

⏰

CAPE is Now Live

On April 20, 2026, CBP launched the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE) system within the ACE Portal. This is the official mechanism for filing refund claims. Phase 1 covers unliquidated entries and entries within 80 days of liquidation.

Who Is Affected

If your company imported goods into the United States and paid tariffs under IEEPA authority, you are entitled to a refund. This includes tariffs imposed on imports from Canada, Mexico, China, and virtually every other country.

🏦 All Importers of Record

Any company that served as the Importer of Record (IOR) on entries subject to IEEPA tariffs. This includes manufacturers, distributors, retailers, and ecommerce sellers who imported goods.

📦 Fentanyl Tariffs (25%)

Tariffs imposed on imports from Canada, Mexico, and China under E.O. 14193 and E.O. 14194, declared in response to illicit drug trafficking emergencies.

🌎 Reciprocal Tariffs (up to 50%)

"Liberation Day" tariffs imposed under E.O. 14257 on imports from virtually every country based on a declared national emergency concerning the U.S. trade deficit.

Critical Deadlines

URGENT

180-Day Protest Window

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3), you have 180 days from the date of liquidation to file a protest. CBP has NOT confirmed whether filing a CAPE Declaration tolls this deadline. File a protest to preserve your rights.

PHASE 1

80-Day CAPE Window

CAPE Phase 1 only covers entries liquidated within the preceding 80 days (approximately January 30 - April 19, 2026). Entries beyond this window must wait for Phase 2 (timeline TBD).

60-90 DAYS

Refund Issuance

CBP has stated that valid refunds will generally be issued within 60-90 days following acceptance of a CAPE Declaration, subject to compliance review.

📣 Confused About Liquidation Dates?

You're not alone. CBP uses intentionally confusing terminology. The 180-day deadline starts from your liquidation date — which is NOT the same as when you paid tariffs or when your goods arrived. This single detail determines whether you can get your refund.

The Problem

Despite the Supreme Court ruling and the CAPE system being live, only 17% of importers are set up to receive refunds. Here's why:

1

Only ~15% Have ACE Portal Accounts

The vast majority of importers do not have an active ACE Portal account with Importer sub-account access, which is required to file CAPE Declarations.

2

ACH Enrollment is Mandatory

Since February 6, 2026, CBP issues ALL refunds via electronic ACH transfer only. No paper checks. You must have U.S. bank account information on file in ACE.

3

Complex Filing Requirements

CAPE Declarations require CSV file uploads with entry numbers, limited to 9,999 entries per file. Only IORs or their licensed customs brokers can file.

4

Deadline Pressure

The 180-day protest deadline continues running. If you miss it, you may lose your right to a refund entirely. Many importers are unaware of this risk.
