The CAPE Refund Process

Step-by-step guide to filing your IEEPA tariff refund claim through CBP's Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries system.

What is CAPE?

"CAPE is designed to consolidate refunds of IEEPA duties including interest rather than processing refunds on an entry-by-entry basis. CBP plans to implement CAPE through a phased development approach, adding more functionality in subsequent phases for more complicated scenarios." — U.S. Customs and Border Protection, IEEPA Duty Refunds Page

CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) is a new functionality within CBP's Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) designed to streamline the submission and processing of IEEPA duty refund requests. Rather than processing refunds entry-by-entry (which CBP estimated would require 4.4 million working hours), CAPE consolidates refunds by IOR number and liquidation date.

Phase 1 Eligibility

CAPE Phase 1 covers approximately 63% of all entries with IEEPA duties. Here's what's included and excluded:

✓ COVERED in Phase 1

  • Unliquidated entries (still open in CBP system)
  • Entries liquidated within the preceding 80 days (approximately January 30 - April 19, 2026)
  • Entries with liquidation status: "suspended," "extended," or "under review"

✗ NOT COVERED in Phase 1

  • Entries flagged for reconciliation or Entry Type 09
  • Entries on a drawback claim
  • Entries covered by an open protest
  • Entries not filed in ACE or without liquidation status
  • Entries subject to AD/CVD pending liquidation
  • Finally liquidated entries beyond 80 days

Step-by-Step Filing Process

1

Prerequisites: ACE Account + ACH Enrollment

Before you can file a CAPE Declaration, you MUST have an ACE Portal account with Importer sub-account view, ACH Refund Authorization completed (bank account on file), and CBP Form 5106 on file with CBP. See our ACE Setup Guide →

2

Prepare Your Entry List

Export entry summaries from ACE (ES-701/ES-702 reports). Identify entry numbers subject to IEEPA tariffs. Each CAPE Declaration is limited to 9,999 entries. File format: CSV only (comma-separated values). The CSV template is available through the CAPE tab in ACE Portal.

3

File CAPE Declaration

Log into ACE Portal. Navigate to the CAPE tab. Click "Upload" button. Click "CAPE Upload Template" to download template. Enter entry numbers in the CSV file. Upload the CSV file. System validates entries: valid entries are accepted, invalid ones are rejected with reason codes. Upon acceptance, you receive a CAPE claim number.

4

CBP Processing

Once accepted: ACE strips the IEEPA Chapter 99 HTS code from each entry. CBP recalculates duties without IEEPA tariffs. Entry is updated to new version. CBP reviews and liquidates/reliquidates. Refunds are consolidated by IOR and liquidation date. Liquidations are processed Monday-Thursday.

5

Refund Issuance

Timeline: 60-90 days after acceptance of CAPE Declaration. Method: Electronic ACH transfer to bank account on file. Interest: Included at applicable rate under 19 U.S.C. § 1505 (6% for individuals, 5% for corporations, 3.5% for corporate overpayments above $10,000). Compliance Review: May extend timeline if concerns arise.

Who Can File?

✓ CAN File

  • The Importer of Record (IOR) who filed the entries
  • The licensed customs broker who filed the entries on behalf of the IOR

✗ CANNOT File

  • Attorneys (must work through IOR or broker)
  • Third-party consultants
  • Trade associations
  • CAPI filings through Automated Broker Interface (ABI)

Critical Strategic Considerations

⚠ The 180-Day Protest Deadline

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3), importers have 180 days from the date of liquidation to file a protest. CBP has NOT confirmed whether filing a CAPE Declaration tolls (stops) this deadline.

Critical Risk: If you file a CAPE Declaration and it's rejected or delayed, and the 180-day protest window expires, you may lose your right to a refund entirely.

Recommendation: File a protest before the 180-day deadline to preserve your rights. Note: entries covered by an open protest are EXCLUDED from Phase 1 CAPE processing.

⏰ The 90-Day Voluntary Reliquidation Window

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1501, CBP has 90 days after liquidation to voluntarily reliquidate entries. Phase 1 was originally designed for entries within this window, but CBP narrowed it to 80 days to allow for processing time.

📋 Entries Beyond 180 Days

The CIT's March 27 order potentially covers finally liquidated entries, but CBP has not built Phase 2 functionality yet. Options: (1) Wait for Phase 2 (timeline unknown); (2) File action at the CIT under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i); (3) File a summons and complaint in the CIT.
