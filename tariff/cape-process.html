The CAPE Refund Process

Step-by-step guide to filing your IEEPA tariff refund claim through CBP's Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries system.

What is CAPE?

"CAPE is designed to consolidate refunds of IEEPA duties including interest rather than processing refunds on an entry-by-entry basis. CBP plans to implement CAPE through a phased development approach, adding more functionality in subsequent phases for more complicated scenarios." — U.S. Customs and Border Protection, IEEPA Duty Refunds Page

CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) is a new functionality within CBP's Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) designed to streamline the submission and processing of IEEPA duty refund requests. Rather than processing refunds entry-by-entry (which CBP estimated would require 4.4 million working hours), CAPE consolidates refunds by IOR number and liquidation date.

What the Heck Is a Liquidation Date?

CBP intentionally uses confusing terminology. Here's what "liquidation" actually means in plain English.

📣 Simple Definition

Liquidation is the moment when Customs & Border Protection finalizes your import entry. It's not when you paid. It's not when the ship docked. It's the date CBP officially closes your file and says "this entry is done." Until then, your entry is "unliquidated" and still open.

📅 When Did I Import?

This is the date your goods physically entered the U.S. and your broker filed the entry summary (CBP Form 7501). You probably paid the IEEPA tariff around this time. This is NOT the liquidation date.

✅ What Is Liquidation?

CBP reviews your entry, verifies duties were paid correctly, checks for compliance issues, and then "liquidates" it. Think of it like a tax audit closing. The liquidation date is the official date CBP stamps your file as "final."

⏰ How Long Does It Take?

By law, CBP has 314 days (about 10 months) from the date of entry to liquidate. Most entries liquidate automatically within that window. You typically receive a CBP Form 4333-A (Notice of Liquidation) in the mail.

Top 5 Questions About Liquidation

How do I find my liquidation dates?

Log into your ACE Portal and run the ES-701 or ES-702 report. Look for the "Liquidation Date" column. If you don't have ACE Portal access yet, ask your customs broker to pull this report for you. This is the single most important piece of information for your refund claim.

What does "unliquidated" mean?

It means CBP hasn't finalized your entry yet. The file is still open. This is GOOD for you. Unliquidated entries are automatically covered by CAPE Phase 1 with no deadline pressure. You don't need to file a protest for unliquidated entries.

Does liquidation have anything to do with my business going bankrupt or closing?

No. "Liquidation" in customs has nothing to do with bankruptcy, going out of business, or selling assets. It's purely a CBP administrative term for finalizing an import entry. CBP chose this confusing word decades ago, and it's designed to make the process opaque.

I paid the tariff six months ago. Was that liquidation?

No. Paying the tariff is just paying the tariff. Liquidation is a separate event that happens later, when CBP formally closes your entry. Many importers paid IEEPA tariffs in February, March, or April 2026, but their entries won't liquidate until late 2026 or early 2027. You need to check.

What happens if I miss the 180-day protest deadline?

After 180 days from liquidation, your entry becomes "finally liquidated." This means CBP considers the matter closed and legally binding. You lose the right to file a protest. Your remaining options are: (1) Wait for CAPE Phase 2 (timeline unknown), or (2) File a lawsuit in the Court of International Trade, which is expensive and complex. Don't miss the deadline.

What Do I Do Now?

The answer depends on your liquidation status, not when you paid or when the goods arrived. Here's every scenario explained.

✓ Scenario 1: Entry is Still Unliquidated (MOST COMMON)

If your entry hasn't been liquidated yet, you are in the best possible position. No deadline pressure. Unliquidated entries are automatically covered by CAPE Phase 1.

Action: Set up your ACE Portal account, enroll in ACH, and file a CAPE Declaration when ready. You have plenty of time. Focus on getting your ACE account set up correctly.

How to check: Look at your ES-701 report. If there's no liquidation date listed, or the status says "unliquidated," "suspended," or "extended," you're in this category.

⚠ Scenario 2: Entry Liquidated Within the Last 80 Days

If your entry was liquidated in the last 80 days (approximately between January 30, 2026 and April 19, 2026), you are covered by CAPE Phase 1.

Action: File a CAPE Declaration IMMEDIATELY. Set up ACE Portal and ACH enrollment as fast as possible. Your entry is eligible, but the sooner you file, the sooner you get your refund.

How to check: Look at the "Liquidation Date" on your ES-701 report. If it's between Jan 30 and Apr 19, 2026, you're here.

⚠ Scenario 3: Entry Liquidated 80-180 Days Ago (THE DANGER ZONE)

This is the most dangerous scenario. Your entry was liquidated more than 80 days ago (before January 30, 2026), so it's NOT covered by CAPE Phase 1. But the 180-day protest window may still be open.

Action (URGENT): Calculate your protest deadline immediately. If you're still within 180 days of liquidation, file a CBP Form 19 Protest right now to preserve your rights. Even if you plan to use CAPE later, filing a protest protects you.

Example: If your entry liquidated on December 1, 2025, your 180-day deadline is May 30, 2026. You must file a protest before May 30 or you lose your right to a refund.

Important: If you file a protest, that entry is EXCLUDED from CAPE Phase 1. But that's okay — a protest preserves your rights, and you can still get your refund through the protest process or CAPE Phase 2.

Scenario 4: Entry Liquidated More Than 180 Days Ago

If your entry liquidated more than 180 days ago, the protest deadline has expired. Your entry is "finally liquidated." This is the hardest position to be in.

Your Options:

  • Option 1: Wait for CAPE Phase 2. CBP has said they "expect to develop" functionality for finally liquidated entries, but there is no timeline.
  • Option 2: File a summons in the Court of International Trade under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i). This is a lawsuit, requires an attorney, and can be expensive.
  • Option 3: File a complaint in the CIT challenging the finality of liquidation. Also requires legal representation.

Bottom line: For entries in this category, your best hope is CAPE Phase 2. The CIT's March 27 order technically covers these entries, but CBP hasn't built the system to process them yet.

How do I know which scenario I'm in?

Pull your ES-701 or ES-702 report from the ACE Portal (or ask your broker). Look at the "Liquidation Date" column for every entry that had IEEPA tariffs. Compare those dates to today. If any entry is in Scenario 3 (liquidated 80-180 days ago), you need to act immediately to file a protest before the deadline expires.

Who Can File?

✓ CAN File

  • The Importer of Record (IOR) who filed the entries
  • The licensed customs broker who filed the entries on behalf of the IOR

✗ CANNOT File

  • Attorneys (must work through IOR or broker)
  • Third-party consultants
  • Trade associations
  • CAPI filings through Automated Broker Interface (ABI)

Critical Strategic Considerations

⚠ The 180-Day Protest Deadline

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1514(c)(3), importers have 180 days from the date of liquidation to file a protest. CBP has NOT confirmed whether filing a CAPE Declaration tolls (stops) this deadline.

Critical Risk: If you file a CAPE Declaration and it's rejected or delayed, and the 180-day protest window expires, you may lose your right to a refund entirely.

Recommendation: File a protest before the 180-day deadline to preserve your rights. Note: entries covered by an open protest are EXCLUDED from Phase 1 CAPE processing.

⏰ The 90-Day Voluntary Reliquidation Window

Under 19 U.S.C. § 1501, CBP has 90 days after liquidation to voluntarily reliquidate entries. Phase 1 was originally designed for entries within this window, but CBP narrowed it to 80 days to allow for processing time.

📋 Entries Beyond 180 Days

The CIT's March 27 order potentially covers finally liquidated entries, but CBP has not built Phase 2 functionality yet. Options: (1) Wait for Phase 2 (timeline unknown); (2) File action at the CIT under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i); (3) File a summons and complaint in the CIT.
