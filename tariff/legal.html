The Constitutional Question

Article I, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution grants Congress the exclusive power "To lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises." The Framers gave Congress alone "access to the pockets of the people" and vested no part of that taxing power in the executive branch.

The President claimed authority under IEEPA's language allowing him to "regulate ... importation" during a national emergency. The Court held that "regulate" does not encompass the power to impose tariffs, and that IEEPA contains no reference to tariffs, duties, or taxes whatsoever.

SCOTUSblog: Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump (Tariffs) — Docket No. 24-1287 — Decided February 20, 2026

"Based on two words separated by 16 others in Section 1702(a)(1)(B) of IEEPA—'regulate' and 'importation'—the President asserts the independent power to impose tariffs on imports from any country, of any product, at any rate, for any amount of time. Those words cannot bear such weight." — Chief Justice John Roberts, Opinion of the Court
"Had Congress intended to convey the distinct and extraordinary power to impose tariffs, it would have done so expressly, as it consistently has in other tariff statutes." — Chief Justice John Roberts, Opinion of the Court
"The terms of IEEPA do not authorize tariffs." — Chief Justice John Roberts, Opinion of the Court

Key Legal Reasoning

📚

Statutory Text

IEEPA (50 U.S.C. § 1701 et seq.) authorizes the President to "investigate, block during the pendency of an investigation, regulate, direct and compel, nullify, void, prevent or prohibit ... importation or exportation." The Court noted that absent from this lengthy list is any mention of tariffs or duties. Congress has consistently conferred tariff authority through express language in other statutes.

⚖

Major Questions Doctrine

The Court applied the major questions doctrine, requiring clear congressional authorization for executive actions of "economic and political significance." The power to impose tariffs of "unlimited amount, duration, and scope" is precisely such a major question requiring express statutory authority.

📋

Historical Practice

No prior President had ever used IEEPA to impose tariffs. Other tariff statutes (Section 232, Section 301, Section 122) contain express limitations and procedural requirements that IEEPA lacks. The Court rejected extending earlier cases (Algonquin SNG, Dames & Moore) as inapplicable.

Court of International Trade Orders

Following the Supreme Court ruling, the U.S. Court of International Trade (CIT) issued a series of orders directing CBP to process refunds. The CIT has exclusive jurisdiction over customs and tariff matters.

📅 March 4, 2026 Order — Atmus Filtration, Inc. v. United States (CIT Case No. 26-01259)

The CIT ordered CBP to: (1) liquidate all unliquidated entries without IEEPA duties; (2) reliquidate all entries for which liquidation is not final with a refund of IEEPA duties; and (3) provide weekly updates on refund system development.

📅 March 6, 2026 Amendment

The CIT suspended the March 4 order "to the extent that it directs immediate compliance" to allow CBP time to develop the CAPE system. CBP filed a declaration describing the proposed four-component CAPE system.

📅 March 27, 2026 Order — Euro-Notions Florida, Inc. v. United States

The CIT further amended its order to include reliquidation of entries for which liquidation has become final—potentially covering all 53 million entries regardless of liquidation status.

📅 April 1, 2026 Amendment

The CIT confirmed that the March 4 order covers finally liquidated entries, but CBP's Phase 1 of CAPE would NOT include these. CBP stated it "expects to develop" functionality for finally liquidated entries in subsequent phases.

📅 April 14, 2026 Order

The CIT ordered CBP to file a report on Phase 1 progress, with a closed conference to follow. The lead case driving the next round of court orders is now AGS Company Automotive Solutions v. CBP.

Tariffs Affected

The Supreme Court ruling invalidated ALL tariffs imposed under IEEPA authority. This includes:

🚁 "Fentanyl Tariffs" — 25%

Imposed on imports from Canada, Mexico, and China under E.O. 14193 and E.O. 14194, declared in response to a national emergency concerning illicit fentanyl entering the United States.

🌎 "Liberation Day" Tariffs — Up to 50%

Reciprocal tariffs imposed under E.O. 14257 on imports from virtually every country based on a declared national emergency concerning the U.S. trade deficit.

📋 Any IEEPA-Based Tariffs

The Court's ruling was categorical: IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs under any circumstances. This forecloses the use of IEEPA as a statutory basis for future tariffs as well.

What This Means for Your Refund:

The Supreme Court only struck down tariffs imposed under IEEPA (the red portion of the bars). Section 301 tariffs (blue) and Section 232 tariffs (gray) were NOT affected by the ruling and remain in place.

If you imported goods during the peak period (April 2025), you paid roughly 164% total. After the Supreme Court ruling, the IEEPA portion (~145%) is eligible for refund. The remaining ~19% (Section 301 + 232) stays.

Source: Congressional Research Service, U.S.-China Tariff Actions Since 2018: An Overview (IF12990), February 2026.

📜 Section 301 (2018-Present)

Initiated under the Trade Act of 1974, Section 301 investigations found China engaged in unfair technology transfer and IP practices. Tariffs were imposed in multiple rounds starting July 2018. These tariffs remain in effect and were NOT challenged in the Supreme Court case.

📜 Section 232 (2018-Present)

Imposed under the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, these tariffs target steel, aluminum, and derivative products from all countries including China. They remain in effect and were not part of the IEEPA ruling.

📜 IEEPA (2025-2026) — STRUCK DOWN

Imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, these included the "Fentanyl Tariffs" and "Liberation Day" reciprocal tariffs. The Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. These are the tariffs being refunded through CAPE.
