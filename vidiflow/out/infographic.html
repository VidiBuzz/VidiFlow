[17] Per-pin data rates: GDDR7 32–48 Gbps/pin (128–192 GB/s per 32-bit device) vs LPDDR5X 6.4–9.6 Gbps/pin (~34 GB/s per 32-bit device). Equal terabytes-per-second totals come from very different underlying fabrics — LPDDR5X trades latency and sustained throughput for energy efficiency.

[18] Qwen3.8-Flash-Next model card: 125B language-model parameters with 6B activated per token, plus a 51B n-gram embedding table and a 4B MTP head; 512 MoE experts with 10 routed + 1 shared active. The "125B" headline counts parameters that do no arithmetic on a given token.

[19] Single-card MoE vs dense throughput sweep (Radeon AI PRO R9700, vLLM 0.16, AWQ 4-bit, May 2026): dense 14B = 7.5 tok/s single-stream vs 30B/3B-active MoE = 51 tok/s single-stream; at 16 concurrent users the MoE holds 381 tok/s aggregate but only 24 tok/s per user. Peer-reviewed follow-up (Lynx, SC’26) finds MoE decode degrades 1.7–3× versus a small dense model as batch size rises, because activated parameters grow toward total parameters.

[20] Unified-memory contention in practice: on an M5 Ultra 256 GB, launching a second copy of a loaded model caused swapping and dropped decode from 100.2 to 12 tok/s. A discrete GPU has dedicated VRAM and no competing consumer for it.
